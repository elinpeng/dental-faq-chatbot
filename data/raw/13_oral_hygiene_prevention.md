# Oral Hygiene and Prevention

## How should I brush my teeth?
A simple Swedish rule for brushing is the 2-2-2 method:
- Brush **2** times a day, morning and evening
- Use **2** centimeters of fluoride toothpaste for adults
- Brush for **2** minutes

Also avoid eating or drinking for 2 hours after brushing in the evening, and do not rinse with water after brushing, only spit. This keeps the fluoride on your teeth longer so it protects better.

## What toothpaste should I use?
Use a toothpaste with fluoride. For adults and children from age 6, a toothpaste with 1,450 ppm fluoride is recommended. People with a high risk of cavities may get a toothpaste with extra high fluoride (5,000 ppm) on prescription from their dentist.

## Is an electric toothbrush better than a manual one?
An electric toothbrush with a rotating head removes slightly more plaque than a manual toothbrush and makes it easier to brush well. A manual toothbrush works well too if you use the right technique. Whichever you use, choose a soft brush, and replace the brush head or toothbrush every three months or when the bristles are worn.

## Should I use dental floss or interdental brushes?
Clean between your teeth once a day, because a toothbrush does not reach there. Choose the tool that fits your gaps:
- **Dental floss or floss holders**: Best for tight spaces between teeth, common in young people
- **Interdental brushes (mellanrumsborstar)**: Remove more plaque in wider gaps and are often recommended for adults, people with gum disease, implants, bridges, or braces. The brush should fit snugly without being forced.
- **Toothpicks**: Can be used in wider gaps

Your dental hygienist can show you which size and tool suits you.

## Do I need mouthwash?
Mouthwash does not replace brushing and cleaning between the teeth. A fluoride mouthwash can be useful for people with a higher risk of cavities, for example people with braces, dry mouth, or many fillings. Antibacterial mouthwash with chlorhexidine is used for short periods on a dentist's advice, since long use can stain the teeth.

## How does diet affect my teeth?
It is how often you eat sugar, not only how much, that matters most. Every time you eat or drink something sugary, the bacteria make acid that attacks the teeth for about 20–30 minutes.
- Eat regular meals and avoid snacking all day long
- Drink water between meals
- Keep sweets to fewer occasions, for example once a week
- Be careful with drinks that contain sugar or acid, like soft drinks, juice, energy drinks, and sports drinks
- Chew sugar-free gum with xylitol after meals to increase saliva

## How often should I go to the dentist in Sweden?
In Sweden, the dental clinic decides how often you need a checkup based on your individual risk of cavities and gum disease. For adults with healthy teeth this is often every 12–24 months, and for people with a higher risk it can be more often. Many checkups are done by a dental hygienist, who examines the teeth and gums, removes tartar, and gives advice. A dentist is consulted when treatment is needed.

## What happens during a dental checkup?
The dentist or dental hygienist checks your teeth, gums, the mucous membranes in your mouth, and your bite. X-rays are often taken every few years to find cavities between the teeth and check the bone. Tartar is removed if needed, and you receive advice about your oral health. The clinic must tell you the price before any treatment.

## What is tartar?
Tartar (tandsten) is plaque that has hardened into a hard crust on the teeth, usually along the gumline and on the inside of the lower front teeth. It cannot be removed by brushing and must be removed at the dental clinic. Good brushing and daily cleaning between the teeth reduce how much tartar forms.

## I am afraid of going to the dentist. What can I do?
Dental fear (tandvårdsrädsla) is common. Tell the clinic about your fear when you book, so they can give you more time and go through everything step by step. You can agree on a stop signal, such as raising your hand. Many clinics offer calming medication or sedation, and some Folktandvården clinics have special teams for people with severe dental fear that offer gradual treatment. People with severe dental fear may be eligible for dental care at the same cost as medical care in some cases.
