# Cavities and Tooth Decay

## What is a cavity?
A cavity is permanent damage to the hard surface of a tooth that develops into tiny holes. It is one of the most common dental problems in Sweden and worldwide. Cavities are caused by bacteria in the mouth that produce acid from sugar, which attacks the tooth enamel over time.

## What causes cavities?
Tooth decay occurs when bacteria in the mouth — primarily Streptococcus mutans — feed on sugars and produce acids. These acids dissolve the minerals in tooth enamel. The Swedish Public Health Agency (Folkhälsomyndigheten) highlights that frequent sugar intake throughout the day is the single biggest dietary risk factor for cavities in Sweden.

## What are the stages of tooth decay?
1. **Initial demineralization**: White spots appear on enamel — the earliest sign. Can be reversed with fluoride.
2. **Enamel decay**: Enamel breaks down further; white spots turn brown or black.
3. **Dentin decay**: Decay reaches the softer dentin layer; tooth becomes sensitive to cold, heat, and sweets.
4. **Pulp involvement**: Bacteria reach the pulp (nerves and blood vessels); significant pain begins.
5. **Abscess**: A pocket of pus forms at the root tip; can cause swelling and fever.

## What are the symptoms of a cavity?
- Toothache or spontaneous pain
- Sensitivity to cold, hot, or sweet foods and drinks
- Visible holes, pits, or dark spots on teeth
- Pain when biting down

## How are cavities treated in Sweden?
- **Fluoride treatment**: For very early cavities (white spots), fluoride varnish applied by a dentist or dental hygienist can reverse the damage without drilling.
- **Fillings**: The most common treatment. The decayed area is removed and filled with tooth-colored composite resin. Available at both Folktandvården and private clinics.
- **Crowns**: For extensively damaged teeth, a crown covers and protects the remaining tooth structure.
- **Root canal**: If decay has reached the pulp. See the root canal section for details.
- **Extraction**: For teeth that cannot be saved.

## How much does a filling cost in Sweden?
Filling costs are partly covered by Sweden's dental subsidy system (tandvårdsbidraget and högkostnadsskydd). A simple filling at Folktandvården typically costs between 500–1,200 SEK depending on size and complexity. Children and young people receive free dental care until 31 December of the year they turn 19.

## How can I prevent cavities?
- Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste (the Swedish Dental Association recommends at least 1,450 ppm fluoride for adults)
- Floss or use interdental brushes once daily
- Limit sugar intake — especially between meals (reducing between-meal snacking is a key focus of Swedish dental health campaigns)
- Drink tap water (Swedish tap water is high quality; fluoride in Sweden comes mainly from toothpaste, not water fluoridation)
- Visit your dentist or dental hygienist regularly — every 1–3 years depending on your individual risk level
- Ask about fluoride varnish treatments if you are at higher risk

## Are cavities common in Sweden?
Dental health in Sweden has improved significantly since the 1970s due to fluoride toothpaste and preventive programs. However, cavities remain common, particularly in children and young adults. Socioeconomic factors play a role — groups with lower income and education have higher rates of untreated decay.

## Can a cavity heal on its own?
Very early-stage cavities (white spot lesions) can remineralize with fluoride and improved oral hygiene. Once a physical hole has formed in the tooth, it cannot heal itself and requires professional treatment.
