# Wisdom Teeth and Tooth Extraction

## What are wisdom teeth?
Wisdom teeth are the third molars, the teeth furthest back in the mouth. Most people have four, one in each corner, but some have fewer or none. They usually come out between ages 17 and 25.

## Do wisdom teeth always need to be removed?
No. In Sweden, wisdom teeth are not removed just in case. They are left in place if they are healthy, have room, and can be kept clean. They are removed if they cause problems.

Common reasons for removing a wisdom tooth:
- Repeated infection of the gum around a partly erupted wisdom tooth (pericoronitis)
- A cavity in the wisdom tooth or in the tooth in front of it that cannot be treated
- Damage to the neighboring tooth
- A cyst around the wisdom tooth
- Gum disease around the wisdom tooth

## What is pericoronitis?
Pericoronitis is an infection of the gum flap over a wisdom tooth that is only partly out. Food and bacteria get stuck under the flap. Symptoms are pain at the back of the mouth, swollen gums, a bad taste, difficulty opening the mouth, and sometimes swelling of the cheek and fever. Rinse with warm salt water, clean the area carefully, and contact a dentist. If you get swelling that spreads, a fever, or difficulty swallowing or breathing, seek emergency care immediately.

## How is a tooth removed?
The area is numbed with local anesthesia so the procedure does not hurt, though you feel pressure. A simple extraction takes a few minutes. A wisdom tooth that is stuck in the bone may require a small surgical procedure where the gum is opened and the tooth is sometimes divided into pieces. This usually takes 20–60 minutes. Difficult wisdom teeth are often removed by a specialist in oral surgery.

## What should I do after a tooth extraction?
- Bite on the gauze for about 30 minutes to stop the bleeding
- Do not rinse your mouth, spit, or drink through a straw for the first day, so the blood clot stays in place
- Avoid hot food and drinks while the area is numb
- Do not smoke or use snus for at least a few days, since it slows healing and increases the risk of complications
- Use a cold pack on the cheek to reduce swelling
- Take pain relief as recommended, usually ibuprofen and/or paracetamol
- Brush your other teeth as normal, and carefully clean near the wound from the second day
- Eat soft food for the first few days
- Raise your head with an extra pillow the first night

## How much pain and swelling is normal?
Pain and swelling are usually worst on the second and third day and then get better. After a wisdom tooth surgery, it is normal to have swelling, stiffness in the jaw, and bruising for about a week.

## What is dry socket?
Dry socket (alveolitis) happens when the blood clot in the hole after an extraction falls out or breaks down. It causes strong, throbbing pain that starts two to four days after the extraction and often spreads toward the ear, along with a bad taste and bad breath. It is more common after removal of lower wisdom teeth and in people who smoke. Contact your dental clinic, which can clean the socket and place a soothing dressing.

## When should I contact the dentist after an extraction?
- Bleeding that does not stop after an hour of biting on gauze
- Pain that gets worse after the third day
- Swelling that gets worse after the third day, or fever
- Numbness in the lip or tongue that remains after the anesthesia should have worn off
- Difficulty swallowing or breathing (seek emergency care immediately)

## What does a tooth extraction cost?
A simple extraction usually costs around 1,000–2,000 SEK and a surgical wisdom tooth removal around 2,500–5,000 SEK, depending on the clinic and difficulty. These are rough guides. Extractions are covered by högkostnadsskyddet, and are free for children and young people until 31 December of the year they turn 19.
