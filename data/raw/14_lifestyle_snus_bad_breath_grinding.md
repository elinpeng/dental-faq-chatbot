# Lifestyle and Common Problems: Snus, Smoking, Bad Breath, Grinding, and Pregnancy

## How does snus affect my teeth and gums?
Snus is common in Sweden and affects the mouth in several ways:
- **Snus lesion**: The area where you place the snus gets a white, wrinkled, or thickened patch on the inside of the lip. It usually goes away if you stop or move the snus to another place.
- **Receding gums**: The gum where the snus is placed can recede permanently, exposing the root of the tooth.
- **Sensitive teeth and cavities**: Exposed roots become sensitive and decay more easily, especially if the snus contains sugar or flavors.
- **Staining**: Snus can stain teeth and fillings.
- **Slower healing**: Nicotine reduces blood flow, which slows healing after extractions and surgery.

Nicotine pouches without tobacco can also cause gum irritation and recession where they are placed. Tell your dentist if you use snus or nicotine pouches so they can check the area.

## How does smoking affect my teeth and gums?
Smoking is the strongest risk factor for gum disease that you can change. Smokers get gum disease more often, lose more bone, and respond worse to treatment. Because smoking reduces bleeding from the gums, it can hide the early warning signs of gum disease. Smoking also stains the teeth, causes bad breath, increases the risk of implant failure, and increases the risk of oral cancer. Quitting improves gum health and treatment results.

## What causes bad breath?
Most bad breath comes from the mouth itself, from bacteria on the back of the tongue, between the teeth, and in gum pockets. Common causes:
- Poor cleaning of the teeth, tongue, and between the teeth
- Gum disease
- Dry mouth (morning breath is common because saliva flow drops during sleep)
- Food like garlic and onion
- Smoking and snus
- Less often, problems in the nose, throat, tonsils, or stomach

## How can I get rid of bad breath?
- Brush twice a day and clean between your teeth every day
- Clean your tongue with a tongue scraper or your toothbrush, especially at the back
- Drink water and chew sugar-free gum if your mouth is dry
- Clean dentures every day
- Stop smoking and using snus
- See a dentist or dental hygienist to check for gum disease and tartar

If bad breath remains even though your mouth is healthy, see a doctor to check the nose, throat, and stomach.

## What is teeth grinding (bruxism)?
Teeth grinding or clenching often happens during sleep without the person noticing. It is often linked to stress. Signs include worn or flat teeth, cracked teeth or fillings, sensitive teeth, sore jaw muscles in the morning, headaches, and clicking or pain in the jaw joint.

## How is teeth grinding treated?
- A bite splint (bettskena), a plastic guard worn at night, protects the teeth from wear. It is made by the dental clinic.
- Jaw exercises and relaxation of the jaw muscles
- Reducing stress
- Being aware of clenching during the day and keeping the teeth apart when relaxed

A bite splint for grinding is covered by högkostnadsskyddet.

## How does pregnancy affect my teeth?
Hormone changes during pregnancy make the gums react more strongly to plaque. Many pregnant women get swollen and bleeding gums (pregnancy gingivitis). Good daily cleaning and professional cleaning help. Vomiting from morning sickness exposes the teeth to stomach acid. After vomiting, rinse with water or a fluoride mouthwash and wait about an hour before brushing.

## Is it safe to go to the dentist when pregnant?
Yes. Checkups, cleaning, and needed treatments like fillings can be done during pregnancy, and local anesthesia is safe. The second trimester is often the most comfortable time for treatment. Dental X-rays can be taken if needed, but non-urgent X-rays are often postponed. Tell your dentist that you are pregnant. Teeth whitening should be postponed until after pregnancy and breastfeeding.
