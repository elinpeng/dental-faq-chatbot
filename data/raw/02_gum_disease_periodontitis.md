# Gum Disease and Periodontal Disease

## What is gum disease?
Gum disease is an infection of the tissues that surround and support the teeth. It starts with inflammation of the gums (gingivitis) and can progress to periodontitis, which destroys the bone and tissue holding teeth in place. It is one of the most common chronic diseases in Sweden and a leading cause of tooth loss in adults.

## What is the difference between gingivitis and periodontitis?
- **Gingivitis**: The earliest stage. Gums are red, swollen, and bleed easily when brushing. No bone loss has occurred yet. Fully reversible with proper cleaning and improved home care.
- **Periodontitis**: The advanced stage. The gum and bone pull away from the teeth forming deep pockets that become infected. Bone and connective tissue are destroyed. Without treatment, teeth loosen and may fall out or need extraction.

## What are the symptoms of gum disease?
- Bleeding gums when brushing or flossing
- Red, swollen, or tender gums
- Persistent bad breath
- Receding gums (teeth look longer than before)
- Loose or shifting teeth
- Pain when chewing
- Visible pus around the gumline

## What causes gum disease?
The primary cause is plaque buildup along and under the gumline. Additional risk factors include: smoking (the strongest modifiable risk factor), diabetes, stress, certain medications that cause dry mouth, and hereditary factors.

## How is gum disease treated in Sweden?
- **Professional cleaning (tandstensborttagning)**: Removal of plaque and tartar above the gumline. Usually performed by a dental hygienist at Folktandvården or a private clinic.
- **Deep cleaning (rotplanering)**: Cleaning below the gumline to remove tartar from root surfaces and help the gum reattach. Done under local anesthesia.
- **Antibiotics**: Sometimes prescribed alongside deep cleaning for severe infections.
- **Surgery**: In advanced cases, flap surgery opens the gum to access and clean deep pockets. Bone grafts may be used to regenerate lost bone.
- **Maintenance therapy**: After treatment, patients typically return every 3–4 months for supportive periodontal therapy.

## Is gum disease treatment covered in Sweden?
Yes. Periodontal treatment is covered under Sweden's dental cost protection system (högkostnadsskydd). This means the state covers a significant portion of costs above certain thresholds. Patients with periodontitis are classified as high-risk and may qualify for more frequent subsidized visits.

## Can gum disease be cured?
Gingivitis is completely reversible. Periodontitis cannot be fully cured — the lost bone does not regenerate — but it can be stabilized and its progression stopped with proper treatment and consistent home care. Lifelong maintenance is essential.

## Is gum disease linked to other health conditions?
Yes. Research consistently links periodontal disease to cardiovascular disease, diabetes, respiratory infections, and adverse pregnancy outcomes. The relationship with diabetes is bidirectional: poorly controlled blood sugar worsens gum disease, and gum disease makes blood sugar harder to manage.

## How can I prevent gum disease?
- Brush twice daily, paying particular attention to the gumline
- Clean between your teeth daily — use dental floss for tight spaces, or interdental brushes (mellanrumsborstar) if the gaps are wider. Interdental brushes remove more plaque in wider spaces, while floss is more effective for tightly packed teeth
- Visit a dental hygienist regularly for professional cleaning
- Quit smoking
- Manage systemic conditions like diabetes
- Use an antibacterial mouthwash if recommended by your dentist
