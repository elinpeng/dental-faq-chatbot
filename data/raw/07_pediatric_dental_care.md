# Pediatric Dental Care in Sweden

## When should my child first see a dentist in Sweden?
In Sweden, all children are automatically enrolled in the public dental care system from birth. The first dental visit is typically offered around age 1–2 through Folktandvården. Parents are contacted by their region's dental service with an invitation. Children and young people receive free dental care until 31 December of the year they turn 19.

## When do baby teeth come in?
Baby teeth typically begin erupting around 6 months of age, starting with the lower front teeth. Most children have all 20 baby teeth by around age 3.

## Why are baby teeth important?
Baby teeth are essential for:
- Chewing food properly and ensuring good nutrition
- Supporting normal speech development
- Holding space in the jaw for the incoming permanent teeth
- If a baby tooth is lost too early due to decay or injury, neighboring teeth can drift and block the permanent tooth from erupting correctly

## When do permanent teeth come in?
Permanent teeth begin erupting around age 6, typically starting with the first molars and lower front incisors. The process continues through to about age 12–13 for most teeth, with wisdom teeth appearing between ages 17–25.

## How should I clean my child's teeth?
- **Before teeth erupt**: Wipe the gums with a clean, damp cloth after feeding
- **First teeth to age 2**: Use a rice-grain-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste (1,000 ppm) and a soft infant toothbrush
- **Ages 2–6**: Use a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste; supervise brushing to ensure they spit rather than swallow
- **Ages 6+**: Encourage brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste; continue supervising until around age 8–9 to ensure technique is correct

## What is baby bottle tooth decay?
Baby bottle tooth decay (early childhood caries) occurs when a baby's teeth are frequently exposed to sugary liquids — including milk, formula, and fruit juice — especially during naps or overnight. Bacteria feed on the sugar and produce acid that rapidly decays the teeth. Never put a baby to sleep with a bottle containing anything other than water.

## Are dental sealants used in Sweden?
Yes. Fissure sealants (fissurförsegling) are thin plastic coatings applied to the chewing surfaces of back teeth to prevent cavities in the deep grooves. Swedish dental guidelines recommend sealants for children and adolescents at risk of cavities. They are part of the preventive program at Folktandvården and are covered under free dental care for children and young people (until the end of the year they turn 19).

## Is fluoride recommended for children in Sweden?
Yes. Fluoride is the most important factor in preventing childhood cavities. Swedish guidelines recommend:
- Fluoride toothpaste from the first tooth
- Fluoride varnish applied by dental staff for higher-risk children
- Fluoride tablets are rarely recommended in Sweden today (toothpaste is sufficient for most children)

## What is free dental care for children and young people in Sweden?
In Sweden, all children and young people receive free dental care until 31 December of the year they turn 19. From the year you turn 20, you start paying for dental care and become eligible for the adult subsidies (tandvårdsbidrag and högkostnadsskydd). Free care is provided through Folktandvården or approved private clinics. Free care includes examinations, preventive treatments, and necessary treatments such as fillings, extractions, and orthodontic assessments. Orthodontic treatment (braces) is also subsidized for young people with clinically significant bite problems.

## What should I do if my child knocks out a tooth?
- **Baby tooth**: Do not attempt to replant it. See a dentist to check for damage to the underlying permanent tooth.
- **Permanent tooth**: This is a dental emergency. Keep the tooth moist (in milk, or between the cheek and gum), do not scrub the root, and see a dentist immediately — ideally within 30 minutes. Prompt action can save the tooth.

## How often should children visit the dentist in Sweden?
Under the public system, Folktandvården schedules children's visits based on their individual risk level — typically every 6–24 months. Higher-risk children are seen more frequently. Parents do not need to book appointments; the clinic sends invitations automatically.

## What is the role of the school dental service (skolstomatologin)?
Historically, Swedish schools had on-site dental clinics. Today, dental care for school-age children is provided by Folktandvården regional clinics, though some regions still offer school-based screenings. The preventive focus — including dietary advice, tooth brushing instruction, and fluoride treatments — remains a core part of the Swedish child dental care model.
