# Teeth Whitening

## What is teeth whitening?
Teeth whitening is a cosmetic treatment that lightens the color of natural teeth using a bleaching agent, usually hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide. The agent breaks down stains inside the enamel and dentin so the teeth look lighter.

## What causes discolored teeth?
- **Surface stains**: Coffee, tea, red wine, tobacco, snus, and some foods
- **Stains inside the tooth**: Aging (enamel gets thinner and the yellow dentin shows more), some medications (for example tetracycline in childhood), too much fluoride during tooth development, and injury to a tooth
- **A single dark tooth**: Often caused by a dead nerve after an injury or a root canal treatment

## What whitening options are available in Sweden?
- **Whitening at the dental clinic**: A dentist applies a stronger whitening gel while protecting the gums. Gives the fastest result, often in one or two visits.
- **Home whitening with custom trays from the dentist**: The clinic makes trays that fit your teeth, and you wear them with whitening gel at home for one to two weeks. This is the most common method in Sweden and gives an even, lasting result.
- **Over-the-counter products**: Strips, gels, and toothpastes sold in shops. They contain very low levels of peroxide and give a limited effect.
- **Whitening toothpaste**: Removes surface stains with mild abrasives but does not change the natural color of the tooth.

## What are the rules for whitening products in Sweden?
Sweden follows EU rules for whitening products:
- Products sold in shops may contain at most 0.1% hydrogen peroxide.
- Products with 0.1–6% hydrogen peroxide may only be used after an examination by a dentist, and the first treatment must be done or supervised by a dentist.
- Products with more than 6% hydrogen peroxide are not allowed for cosmetic whitening.
- Whitening with peroxide products above 0.1% is not allowed for people under 18.

Be careful with whitening products bought online from outside the EU, as they may contain much higher levels and damage teeth and gums.

## How much does teeth whitening cost in Sweden?
Prices vary between clinics. As a rough guide, home whitening with custom trays usually costs around 2,000–4,000 SEK, and whitening at the clinic usually costs around 3,000–6,000 SEK. Whitening is a cosmetic treatment, so it is not covered by tandvårdsbidraget or högkostnadsskyddet, and you pay the full cost yourself.

## Is teeth whitening safe?
Yes, when done correctly after a dental examination. The most common side effect is temporary sensitivity in the teeth, which usually goes away a few days after treatment ends. Some people get mild irritation of the gums. Correct whitening does not damage the enamel.

## Who should not whiten their teeth?
- People under 18
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- People with untreated cavities or gum disease (these should be treated first)
- People with very sensitive teeth or exposed root surfaces

## Does whitening work on fillings, crowns, and veneers?
No. Whitening only works on natural teeth. Fillings, crowns, and veneers keep their original color, so they may look darker than the teeth around them after whitening. They may need to be replaced to match.

## How long does the result last?
Usually one to three years, depending on habits. Coffee, tea, red wine, tobacco, and snus make the color return faster. A short repeat treatment with your trays can refresh the result.

## How can I keep my teeth white after whitening?
- Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste
- Cut down on coffee, tea, red wine, tobacco, and snus
- Rinse your mouth with water after drinking staining drinks
- Get regular professional cleaning to remove surface stains
