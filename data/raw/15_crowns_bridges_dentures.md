# Crowns, Bridges, Veneers, and Dentures

## What is a dental crown?
A crown is a cap that covers the whole visible part of a damaged tooth. It is used when a tooth is too broken down for a filling, after a root canal treatment on a back tooth, or to protect a cracked tooth. Crowns are usually made of porcelain or zirconia, which look like natural teeth, or sometimes of metal with porcelain on top.

## How is a crown made?
At the first visit the dentist numbs the tooth, shapes it, and takes an impression or a digital scan. A temporary crown is placed. The final crown is made in a dental laboratory and cemented at a second visit, usually one to three weeks later. Some clinics can make crowns in a single visit with a digital machine.

## How much does a crown cost in Sweden?
A crown usually costs around 6,000–10,000 SEK depending on the clinic and material. This is a rough guide. Crowns are covered by högkostnadsskyddet. For people born in 1959 or earlier, crowns on the ten front teeth in each jaw are covered by tiotandvård, meaning the patient pays 10% of the reference price.

## How long does a crown last?
A crown often lasts 10–15 years or longer with good care. The tooth under the crown can still get cavities at the edge, so brushing and cleaning between the teeth is still important.

## What is a dental bridge?
A bridge replaces one or more missing teeth by attaching an artificial tooth to crowns on the teeth on each side of the gap. A bridge is fixed and cannot be taken out. The downside is that the neighboring teeth must be ground down to hold the crowns. A bridge can also be attached to implants.

## What are veneers?
Veneers (fasader) are thin shells of porcelain or composite glued to the front of the front teeth. They change the color, shape, or size of teeth. When done only for appearance, veneers are cosmetic treatment and are not covered by högkostnadsskyddet.

## What are dentures?
Dentures (tandproteser) are removable artificial teeth.
- **Full denture (helprotes)**: Replaces all the teeth in a jaw. It sits on the gum, and an upper denture is held by suction against the palate. A lower full denture is often less stable.
- **Partial denture (delprotes)**: Replaces some missing teeth and is held by metal clasps on the remaining teeth.
- **Implant-supported denture**: A denture that snaps onto two or more implants. It sits much more firmly, especially in the lower jaw.

## How do I take care of my dentures?
- Take the denture out and clean it every day with a denture brush and mild soap or denture cleaner, not ordinary toothpaste, which scratches it
- Clean over a sink filled with water so the denture does not break if dropped
- Brush your remaining teeth, gums, and tongue
- Many people take the denture out at night to let the gums rest; keep it in water
- See a dentist if the denture becomes loose or causes sores. The jaw changes over time, so dentures often need to be adjusted or relined

## What are the options for replacing a missing tooth?
- **Implant with crown**: Most like a natural tooth and does not affect neighboring teeth, but costs the most and takes several months.
- **Bridge**: Fixed and quicker than an implant, but requires grinding down the neighboring teeth.
- **Partial denture**: Cheapest and removable, but less comfortable and stable.
- **Leaving the gap**: Sometimes acceptable, especially at the back of the mouth, but neighboring teeth can move into the gap.

Your dentist can tell you which options fit your situation and what they cost before you decide.
