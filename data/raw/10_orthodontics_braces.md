# Orthodontics: Braces and Clear Aligners

## What is orthodontic treatment?
Orthodontic treatment moves teeth and corrects the bite using braces, clear aligners, or other appliances. It is done by a specialist in orthodontics (ortodontist) or by a general dentist for simpler cases.

## What problems can orthodontic treatment fix?
- Crowded or crooked teeth
- Gaps between teeth
- Large overbite (upper front teeth stick out far in front of the lower teeth)
- Underbite (lower teeth in front of the upper teeth)
- Crossbite (upper and lower teeth do not meet correctly from side to side)
- Open bite (front teeth do not meet when the back teeth are together)
- Teeth that cannot come out by themselves, for example a stuck canine

## Are braces free for children in Sweden?
Orthodontic treatment can be free for children and young people as part of free dental care, which lasts until 31 December of the year they turn 19. The treatment must be judged necessary for health reasons, not only for appearance. The dental clinic assesses how great the need is, and each region decides which levels of need it treats. Children with severe bite problems are prioritized, while minor cosmetic problems are usually not treated for free.

Your child's regular dental clinic follows the development of the teeth and bite and refers the child to an orthodontist if treatment may be needed. Most assessments happen between ages 9 and 13.

## What does orthodontic treatment cost for adults in Sweden?
Adults usually pay the full cost of orthodontic treatment themselves. Prices vary a lot depending on the problem and the method. As a rough guide, clear aligners or fixed braces for an adult often cost between 25,000 and 60,000 SEK for a full treatment, and simpler treatments of only the front teeth can cost less. Treatment done only for appearance is not covered by högkostnadsskyddet. The clinic must give you a written cost estimate before treatment starts.

## What types of braces are there?
- **Fixed metal braces**: Small brackets glued to the teeth and connected by a wire. Work for almost all problems and are the most common choice for children.
- **Ceramic braces**: Same as metal braces but with tooth-colored brackets, so they are less visible.
- **Lingual braces**: Brackets glued to the inside of the teeth so they cannot be seen. Cost more and can affect speech at first.
- **Clear aligners (for example Invisalign)**: Clear plastic trays changed every one to two weeks. They are almost invisible and can be taken out to eat and brush, but must be worn 20–22 hours a day to work. Best for mild to moderate problems.
- **Removable appliances**: Often used for children while the jaw is still growing.

## How long does treatment take?
Most treatments take between one and two and a half years. Short treatments of only the front teeth can take a few months. The time depends on how much the teeth need to move and how well the patient follows instructions.

## Does it hurt to have braces?
The teeth usually feel sore for a few days after the braces are put on and after each adjustment. Paracetamol or ibuprofen and soft food help. The brackets can rub against the inside of the cheeks at first, and orthodontic wax can be placed over them to protect the cheek.

## How do I take care of my teeth with braces?
- Brush carefully after meals with fluoride toothpaste, around every bracket and along the gumline
- Use interdental brushes to clean under the wire and between the teeth
- An extra fluoride mouth rinse is often recommended, because braces increase the risk of cavities
- Avoid hard and sticky foods that can break brackets, such as hard candy, toffee, and nuts
- Limit sugary snacks and drinks between meals

## What is a retainer and why do I need it?
After braces, the teeth try to move back to where they were. A retainer holds them in their new position. It can be a thin wire glued to the back of the front teeth (permanent retainer) or a removable plastic tray worn at night. Many orthodontists recommend using a retainer for many years, often for life, to keep the result.

## Can adults get braces?
Yes. Teeth can be moved at any age as long as the gums and bone are healthy. Gum disease must be treated before orthodontic treatment starts. Treatment can take a little longer for adults.
