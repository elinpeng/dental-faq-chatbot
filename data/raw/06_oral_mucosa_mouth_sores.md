# Oral Mucosa and Mouth Sores

## What is the oral mucosa?
The oral mucosa is the moist tissue lining the inside of the mouth, including the cheeks, lips, tongue, floor of the mouth, and the roof of the mouth (palate). It acts as a protective barrier and plays a role in taste, digestion, and immune defense. Changes in the oral mucosa can be a sign of local problems or systemic disease.

## What are aphthous ulcers (mouth ulcers)?
Aphthous ulcers, commonly called canker sores, are small, painful sores that form inside the mouth on soft tissue. They are not contagious and are not caused by a virus. They appear as round or oval whitish-yellow sores with a red border and typically heal on their own within 1–2 weeks.

Triggers include:
- Minor injuries (biting the cheek, rough food)
- Stress and fatigue
- Certain foods (citrus fruits, spicy food)
- Hormonal changes
- Nutritional deficiencies (iron, B12, folate)
- Some systemic conditions (celiac disease, Crohn's disease)

Treatment is usually not necessary. For painful or large ulcers, a corticosteroid gel or antimicrobial mouthwash (chlorhexidine) can help speed healing and reduce pain.

## What is oral herpes (cold sores)?
Cold sores are caused by the herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1). They typically appear on or around the lips as clusters of small blisters that burst and form a crust. Unlike aphthous ulcers, cold sores are contagious. The virus stays in the body permanently and can reactivate due to sun exposure, stress, illness, or fatigue.

Antiviral creams (aciclovir) applied at the first tingling sensation can shorten the outbreak. Severe or frequent outbreaks can be managed with oral antiviral medication on prescription.

## What is oral candidiasis (thrush)?
Oral thrush is a fungal infection caused by Candida albicans. It presents as white, creamy patches on the tongue, inner cheeks, or palate that can be wiped off, often leaving a red or bleeding surface beneath. It is more common in infants, the elderly, people wearing dentures, those on antibiotics or inhaled corticosteroids, and immunocompromised individuals.

Treatment involves antifungal medication (nystatin suspension or miconazole gel). The underlying cause should also be addressed — for example, rinsing the mouth after using an asthma inhaler can prevent steroid-induced thrush.

## What is geographic tongue?
Geographic tongue is a harmless condition where smooth red patches with irregular white borders appear on the tongue, resembling a map. The patches change shape and location over time. It may cause mild sensitivity to spicy or acidic foods in some people. No treatment is required, though the cause is not fully understood.

## What is dry mouth (xerostomia)?
Dry mouth is a condition where the salivary glands do not produce enough saliva. Saliva is essential for neutralizing acids, washing away food particles, and preventing tooth decay. Dry mouth significantly increases the risk of cavities and gum disease.

Causes include:
- Certain medications (antihistamines, antidepressants, blood pressure medications — very common)
- Radiation therapy to the head and neck
- Sjögren's syndrome (an autoimmune condition)
- Mouth breathing
- Dehydration

Management includes drinking water frequently, using saliva substitutes, chewing sugar-free gum to stimulate saliva, and using high-fluoride toothpaste to protect against increased cavity risk.

## What is oral cancer and when should I be concerned?
Oral cancer can develop on the tongue, lips, floor of the mouth, cheeks, gums, or palate. Early signs include:
- A sore or ulcer that does not heal within 2–3 weeks
- A red or white patch in the mouth
- A lump or thickening of tissue
- Difficulty chewing, swallowing, or moving the jaw or tongue
- Unexplained numbness in the mouth

Risk factors include tobacco use, heavy alcohol consumption, HPV infection, and prolonged sun exposure to the lips. In Sweden, dentists are trained to screen for oral cancer at routine checkups. Any suspicious lesion that does not heal within 2–3 weeks should be evaluated promptly.

## What are denture sores?
Denture sores are painful ulcers that develop under ill-fitting dentures due to pressure and friction. See your dentist to have the denture adjusted or relined. Never ignore persistent sores under a denture — chronic irritation from an ill-fitting denture is a risk factor for oral cancer.

## Should I see a dentist or a doctor for mouth sores?
For sores inside the mouth, a dentist is usually the best first point of contact. If the sore is on or around the lips, a doctor (or dermatologist) may be more appropriate. If a sore does not heal within 2–3 weeks, always seek professional evaluation regardless of whether it is painful.
